Meet Cynthia

Rooted in community. Ready to serve Ward 2.

Cynthia McCutcheon — Gimiwon Kwe, “Rain Woman” — is an Anishinaabe Kwe from Red Rock Indian Band who lives and works in Sault Ste. Marie. Her life and work have centred on health, culture, education, accessibility, community connection, and creating spaces where people feel seen and supported.

Cynthia holds a Master of Public Health and works in health promotion with Maamwesying North Shore Community Health Services. Her work has included community-based wellness and prevention initiatives with First Nation communities and Indigenous organizations. She has also facilitated Indigenous learning through Sault College, including Let’s Start Ojibwe and Songs & Stories for Early Childhood Education, and she is the owner and instructor behind Dagokiing Yoga, where her training includes trauma-informed movement, yoga and Pilates, older-adult programming, and adaptations for different ages and abilities.

Her community service reaches well beyond one role. Cynthia has contributed to Thinking Rock Community Arts, has served with NORDIK Institute, and has worked as a Knowledge Keeper and workshop facilitator with the Native Women’s Association of Canada. She has supported cultural and wellness programming in Sault Ste. Marie — from accessible yoga in the Downtown Plaza to Indigenous craft and medicine workshops — and has participated in Two-Spirit community organizing in Bawating. Those experiences have put her in rooms with families, Elders, youth, artists, health workers, volunteers, small organizations, and people navigating systems that do not always work as well as they should.

Why Ward 2

Representation has to lead to action.

Cynthia is running because Indigenous voices belong at the municipal decision-making table — and because Ward 2 residents deserve a councillor who is accessible, listens before deciding, and follows through. Housing, roads, public safety, accessibility, economic development, recreation, land and water, and the quality of neighbourhood public spaces are all local-government issues that shape everyday life.

Her approach is people first: bring more voices into the room, ask practical questions, connect major projects to local benefits, and make City Hall easier for residents to understand and reach.

Contribution information

Ontario municipal campaign contributions to a candidate may only be accepted from eligible individuals who are normally resident in Ontario. The maximum contribution to one council candidate is $1,200, and the total contribution limit to candidates running for the same council is $5,000. Contributions over $25 cannot be made in cash. If a contributor gives more than $100 in total, their name and address are reported on the candidate’s public financial statement. Municipal campaign contributions are not tax deductible. Privacy & electoral informationCampaign Privacy Policy ⌄

1. Scope and our approach

This policy describes how the Cynthia McCutcheon Ward 2 campaign handles personal information collected through this campaign website, direct campaign communications, volunteer and lawn-sign requests, and campaign contribution administration.

The campaign is a municipal election campaign, not a government institution. Canadian federal private-sector privacy law is primarily directed at commercial activity and does not generally regulate ordinary non-commercial political activity. The campaign nevertheless adopts privacy practices based on Canadian fair-information principles and complies with the specific election-law rules that apply to municipal campaigns in Ontario.

2. Information we may collect

Depending on how you interact with the campaign, we may collect:

  • your name, email address, postal code and any message you send through the website;
  • volunteer interests, event participation, lawn-sign requests and other campaign preferences you choose to provide;
  • records of direct communications with the campaign;
  • where you make or inquire about a campaign contribution, the information required to determine eligibility, issue a receipt, maintain campaign records and complete the candidate’s financial statement, including contributor name, address, contribution amount and date;
  • limited technical information generated by web hosting or form-processing services, such as IP address, browser information, date/time and security logs.
This site does not currently use behavioural advertising pixels or campaign-operated analytics cookies.

3. How we use information

We use information for legitimate campaign and electoral purposes, including to:

  • respond to questions, requests and Ward 2 issues;
  • coordinate volunteers, events, canvassing and lawn signs;
  • send campaign information or updates where requested or reasonably connected to your campaign interaction;
  • administer permitted campaign contributions and meet record-keeping and financial-reporting obligations;
  • protect the website and campaign systems from fraud, spam and misuse;
  • comply with Ontario election law and other applicable legal obligations.

We do not sell or rent personal information.

4. Ontario municipal voters’ list information

If the campaign receives the Ward 2 portion of the municipal voters’ list from the Clerk under the Municipal Elections Act, 1996, that information is handled separately from ordinary website submissions.

  • The voters’ list is used only for electoral purposes and not for commercial purposes.
  • It is shared only as permitted by the Act and only after the required written acknowledgements are obtained.
  • The campaign tracks persons who receive copies.
  • Physical and electronic copies are returned or securely destroyed as required.
  • The candidate must destroy the voters’ list copies by the end of the campaign period. Written acknowledgements and destruction confirmations are retained for the statutory period.
The campaign does not publish the municipal voters’ list on this website and does not intentionally submit voters’ list data through the website contact form.

5. Contributions and public financial reporting

Ontario municipal-election law requires candidates to keep detailed campaign financial records. The campaign records contributor information needed to assess contribution eligibility and meet those obligations.

If an individual’s contributions to the campaign total more than $100, the contributor’s name and address must be reported on the candidate’s financial statement. The financial statement is a public document. Campaign financial records must be retained through the period required by Ontario election law.

6. Campaign email and electronic communications

Most political messages are not commercial electronic messages for purposes of Canada’s Anti-Spam Legislation, and contribution solicitations by political candidates are generally excluded from the consent requirement in section 6 of CASL. As a campaign practice, we will nevertheless honour reasonable requests to stop receiving campaign email or text updates.

You can withdraw from campaign updates at any time by replying with a request to unsubscribe or emailing 2026itstime@gmail.com.

7. Service providers and data location

This website is hosted using GitHub Pages, a service provided by GitHub, Inc., and uses FormSubmit to process website form submissions. GitHub, FormSubmit, email providers and other campaign service providers may process technical or submitted information on systems located outside Ontario or outside Canada, where it may be subject to the laws of that jurisdiction. The campaign limits the information requested through the website to information reasonably needed for campaign, electoral and contribution-administration purposes.

Do not use the public website form to send highly sensitive information. Contact the campaign directly if a matter requires a more appropriate communication channel.

8. Retention and deletion

Ordinary website inquiries, volunteer information and campaign-contact records are kept only as long as reasonably needed for the campaign, follow-up, compliance and orderly campaign close-out, after which they are deleted, anonymized or securely archived as appropriate.

Records that Ontario election law requires the campaign to keep are retained for the applicable statutory period. Voters’ list copies are subject to the separate destruction requirements described above.

9. Safeguards

The campaign uses reasonable administrative and technical safeguards appropriate to the nature of the information, including limiting access to campaign personnel and volunteers who need the information, using password-protected accounts, avoiding unnecessary collection, and separating election-law records from public website content.

No internet transmission or electronic storage system can be guaranteed to be completely secure.

10. Your choices and requests

You may contact the campaign to ask what information you have provided directly through this website, request a correction, withdraw consent for future campaign updates, or ask that ordinary campaign-contact information be deleted where it is no longer needed.

Some information cannot be deleted on request where the campaign must retain it to comply with election law, financial reporting, legal obligations or the integrity of campaign records.

11. External links

This website links to the City of Sault Ste. Marie, social-media platforms and other third-party services. Those sites operate under their own privacy practices. The campaign is not responsible for third-party privacy policies or content.
